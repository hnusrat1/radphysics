I simulated the 200 events with a Monte Carlo I wrote for this task, since no transport code was available. The sample is one random realization. The cross sections are approximate, so the process mix and spectra are estimates rather than NIST-accurate values.

**Method**

- Source photons were sampled uniformly in solid angle over the 10×10 cm field at SSD 100 cm, with 1.1732 and 1.3325 MeV equally likely.
- Transport used Klein-Nishina Compton, approximate Rayleigh, pair production (annihilation photons tracked) and photoelectric absorption with approximate water coefficients. Photons below 0.1 MeV were absorbed.
- The cylinder (r = 1 cm, z = 4.9–5.1 cm) was forced: each photon's first interaction inside it was recorded with its path probability as weight, and the photon continued with the complementary weight.
- About 3.2×10⁵ weighted events were resampled with replacement in proportion to weight. The effective sample size was about 1.8×10⁵, so the 200 events are essentially distinct.

**Checks**

- The Klein-Nishina cross section reproduces the NIST value at 0.5 MeV (0.0967 cm²/g).
- The unscattered-primary share of the weighted events (~54%) agrees with the analytic estimate for primary interactions in the cylinder.
- In this draw: 199 Compton, 1 Rayleigh, 0 pair, 0 photoelectric. Expected shares are roughly 99.2% Compton, 0.5% Rayleigh, 0.14% pair and 0.18% photoelectric, so the absence of pair and photoelectric events is consistent with chance.

**Simplifications**

- No bremsstrahlung, fluorescence, incoherent scattering function, or electron transport.
- Pair and photoelectric cross sections are crude.
- Electron directions for pair and photoelectric events are approximate.
- Only the first interaction in the cylinder is recorded (second-order re-interactions are neglected).
