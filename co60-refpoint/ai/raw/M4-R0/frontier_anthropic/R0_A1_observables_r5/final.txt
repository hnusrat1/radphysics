I ran out of tool budget before finishing, so this answer is incomplete. Layers 1–2 are computed from my own simplified transport model. Several electron-level and dose values are rough estimates, and one consistency check failed. The main gaps are listed below.

**What is computed**
- **Layers 1–2 (photons):** A point-source, pyramid-sampled Monte Carlo over 1.6×10⁷ histories with track-length tallies in the 1 cm radius, 0.2 cm thick scoring cylinder. The primary value matches the analytic exp(−μ·5 cm) to 0.3%.
- **Kerma:** K/Φ0 = 5.36×10⁻¹² Gy cm², from the Klein–Nishina energy-transfer cross section plus a photoelectric term.

**What is estimated or incomplete**
- **Layer 3 spectra and direction:** The per-bin arrays and direction distribution are hand-built shape estimates, not tallied output. Only the mean energies (0.355 MeV all electrons, 0.587 MeV primary-parented) come from the calculation.
- **Layer 4:** The fluence (0.0152 per Φ0), delta fraction (0.032), mean energy (0.438 MeV) and radiative-to-collision ratio (0.00258) come from a continuous-slowing-down model. Its energy-balance check failed by about 20% (the restricted-stopping integral gives 0.041 MeV/cm against a photon kerma of 0.0335 MeV/cm), so the electron fluence and its spectrum are probably overestimated. The angular distribution is an assumed mixture, not computed.
- **Layer 5:** D and K_col use CPE with β = 1 and g ≈ 0.0032. The Spencer–Attix s_w,air (about 1.12) and PDD(5 cm) (about 81%) are estimates; I did not finish the depth-dose or restricted-stopping calculation. The parent-scattered dose fraction (0.35) is also an estimate.

**Stopping-power and cross-section inputs**
Klein–Nishina Compton with no binding correction. Collision stopping power is Bethe with I = 75 eV, which matches ESTAR to about 1% at 0.01–10 MeV. Radiative stopping power is an empirical ratio of 0.0075·T, consistent with ESTAR-level values near 1 MeV. Photoelectric and coherent cross sections are power-law approximations, and pair production is a rough near-threshold guess. Pair and Rayleigh fractions in particular are approximate.
