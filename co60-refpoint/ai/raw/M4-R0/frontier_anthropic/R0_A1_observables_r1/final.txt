These values are estimates from a simplified calculation, not a full Monte Carlo transport. I wrote a photon transport with next-event estimation to the scoring point, plus an analytic slowing-down model for the electrons. The primary fluence and the dose-level quantities should be good to about 1–2%. The scatter, spectral and electron quantities are less certain, roughly 5–20%.

Key results:
- Primary fluence at 5 cm is 0.729 Φ0, and the total is 1.15 Φ0. The scattered fraction is 0.37 by fluence and 0.18 by energy fluence.
- Compton scattering dominates the interactions at the point (about 99%).
- Dose per Φ0 is about 5.35×10⁻¹² Gy cm². About 19% of this comes from electrons whose parent photon was scattered.
- PDD(5 cm) is about 80.5%, with dmax taken as 0.5 cm.

Main approximations:
- Cross sections are Klein–Nishina for Compton, with no binding or incoherent scattering function. Photoelectric, coherent and pair cross sections are crude power-law fits. I did not use NIST XCOM or ESTAR tables.
- Stopping powers are Bethe/Berger–Seltzer with a Sternheimer density effect. The radiative stopping power is a crude fit. The Møller delta-ray shape is approximate.
- Electrons are not transported. Layers 3–4 come from a continuous slowing-down model fed by the photon arrivals. Electron angular distributions use the photon-born directions as a proxy.
- Dose is set equal to collision kerma (charged-particle equilibrium), so β = 1.
- Water stopping-power ratio s_w,air uses the approximate restricted stopping powers above, so it is less reliable than the others.
