At which step of the photon-to-dose chain do AI answers fail?

The Monte Carlo side of the study is built. For every step from the incident photon to the absorbed dose at 5 cm depth in a Co-60 beam, there is now a reference value with a known uncertainty and an independent check. This page explains what was done, what the numbers say, where the reference is solid and where it is not, and where the work could be published.

In short

Status

M0 and M1 are complete and reviewed. All plan targets are met. One approved extension run is tightening the scoring-boundary bound.

Headline numbers

D/Φ₀ = 5.3584 × 10⁻¹² Gy cm² ± 0.066%, β = 1.0045, sw,air = 1.1268 (ICRU 90) / 1.1331 (ICRU 37), PDD(5) = 81.5%.

What matters beyond this study

The stopping-power basis moves sw,air by up to 1%, far more than any Monte Carlo uncertainty. Geant4 option4 drops Rayleigh scattering above 1.022 MeV in its default gamma process. A water–water scoring boundary distorts the dose next to it by a few percent.

Still open

EGSnrc and the other Geant4 lists (M2); your verification of refs.yaml; the Fano step-size diagnostics; the boundary systematic on D, now ±0.17% and shrinking.

Why this study

Language models state textbook radiation physics fluently. Stating a cross section is a different skill from modelling what photons and electrons do at a point in a phantom, and a check on the final dose cannot tell the two apart: a model can recall a PDD table and still have the transport wrong. The study therefore grades every intermediate quantity in the photon-to-dose chain against a Monte Carlo that scores the same quantity in the same bins, and asks at which layer an answer first leaves tolerance.

The point is the Co-60 reference condition: central axis, 5 cm depth in water, 10 × 10 cm² at SSD 100 cm, where standards laboratories calibrate chambers in ND,w. Its protocol quantities (sw,air from TRS-398, β slightly above 1, PDD tables) are in every model's training data. The design uses that: it compares a recalled quantity (PDD(5)) with a computed one at the same point (D/Φ₀).

Hypotheses, recorded before any data. H1: errors are small for primary-photon quantities, which follow from μ and two-body kinematics. H2: errors are larger for the scattered photon spectrum and the electron fluence spectrum, which need transport. H3: models reproduce PDD(5) better than D/Φ₀. The spread between Monte Carlo codes sets the resolution: an AI difference inside it is reported as unresolved.

How the reference was built

  • Own application, no TOPAS or GATE. A Geant4 11.4.3 application (option4, 10 keV production thresholds in the whole phantom) in which every scorer is visible: point source model A uniform over the 10 × 10 cm² pyramid, 30 cm water cube, scoring cylinder r = 1 cm, t = 0.2 cm at 5 cm.
  • Histories are tagged. Every photon carries its category (primary per line, scattered source photon, secondary photon); every electron carries its parent's category and a delta-ray flag. That is what lets each layer be split by primary and scattered parents.
  • Estimators. Track length for photon and electron fluence, with electron track length spread over each step's energy interval by the restricted CSDA range. Kerma from the energy transferred at each interaction, not from tabulated μtr. Dose from deposition. sw,air by Spencer–Attix from the scored electron spectrum with the Nahum track-end term, on four stopping-power bases.
  • Variance reduction. Electron Russian roulette outside a region of interest sized at 1.2 × the CSDA range, and photon importance sampling in a parallel world. Together they give about 25× in efficiency for D. An analog run checks them.
  • Statistics. Independent batches, plus per-history second moments where available. Every quoted error is the larger of the two (D023).
  • Provenance. Pinned builds, a manifest for every run, frozen production binaries tagged with their source commit, every run kept (superseded ones archived with a reason), and every choice logged in DECISIONS.md.

Reading the numbers

  • Scatter is the story at 5 cm. A third of the photons reaching the point have scattered, and electrons set in motion by them deposit 18.8% of the dose. A model that treats the beam as primary-only will be off by about a fifth in dose. That is a large, unambiguous signal against a 2% tolerance.
  • Compton carries the energy, but not all the interactions. 96.5% of interactions and 99.8% of the energy transfer are Compton, yet the softened scattered spectrum makes photoelectric absorption 2.4% of interactions. A model reasoning from the primary lines alone will put that near zero.
  • β = 1.0045. Dose exceeds collision kerma by 0.45% at 5 cm, as transient CPE beyond dmax requires; the plan expected an excess on the order of 0.5%.
  • sw,air depends on the data more than on the transport. The same electron spectrum gives 1.1268 with ICRU 90 stopping powers and 1.1331 with ICRU 37. The ICRU 37 value matches the TRS-398 figure of 1.133 (in refs.yaml, not yet verified). Scoring uses ICRU 90 as primary and reports ICRU 37 alongside (D024), so a model quoting the protocol is not marked wrong for quoting it.
  • Bound-electron Compton matters for the event task. option4 uses a bound-electron Compton model, so recoil energies are smeared around the free-electron edge. The validity rules for sampled events allow a Doppler tolerance of 60 keV (the 99.9th percentile of the Monte Carlo departure). Under the strict free-electron rules, 99% of Monte Carlo events would fail on energy conservation alone, because binding energy goes to local deposition.

What went wrong, and what it taught us

Geant4 option4 drops Rayleigh scattering above 1.022 MeV

The default G4GammaGeneralProcess does not sample coherent scattering in its 2mec²–100 MeV zone, which holds both Co-60 lines. The first production run showed zero Rayleigh events among primaries. The dose effect is small, but Layer 2 interaction shares would have been wrong. Production now switches the general process off (D019). This should go to the Geant4 developers as an issue report.

The plan's linear electron-fluence estimator fails energy closure by 6.4%

Geant4 ends an electron below about 20 keV in a single step, over which the path per unit energy is 1/L(E), not constant. Spreading track length linearly puts too much fluence at low energy: the dose rebuilt from the spectrum is 6.4% high. Weighting by the restricted CSDA range closes to 0.07%. In the 10–20 keV bin the two differ by 9%, which matters for the lowest comparison bins; sw,air moves by less than 0.01% (D020).

A water–water boundary distorts the dose next to it

The depth scan shows the dose up to 3.8% high 1–4 mm in front of the scoring cylinder and 2.2% low behind it, with water on both sides. Moving the cylinder to 8 cm moves the pattern; far from both cylinders the scans agree to +0.11%. The likely cause is GS multiple scattering with the SafetyPlus step limit: steps shrink near a boundary, and a step-length-dependent displacement of deposition piles energy up in front of it. Inside the cylinder the dose is 0.9974 ± 0.0017 of the boundary-free value. That is not significant, but the bound is still wider than D's statistical error, so it is carried as a systematic and tightened by the running extension (D025). From M2, dose is scored without a boundary near R0, partly because EGSnrc crosses boundaries with a different algorithm.

A 4σ anomaly that was a fluctuation

The main run's primary fluence came out 0.12% below exp(−μs)/(ΩR²) at 4.4σ on its batch spread. Eight diagnostic runs (photon-only and full transport, with and without importance sampling, two geometries), a transmission measurement of the tracking μ and a collision-estimator cross-check found no cause. The main run's batches simply agreed with each other more than their per-history variance allowed. A replicate with new seeds, pooled by a rule fixed before it finished (D022), came out normal; pooled, the check gives 0.9992 ± 0.0004. The lasting change is the uncertainty policy (D023).

Geant4's own water stopping power is not Bethe–Møller below 100 keV

option4 uses the Penelope ionisation model below 100 keV. For water it is 2.5% above Bethe–Møller (I = 78 eV) at 12 keV and 1.7% at 100 keV, and Geant4 smooths that offset into the higher-energy model as 1.7% × (100 keV/E). Air agrees within 0.1%. That is why the Geant4-basis sw,air is 1.1385, 1% above the ICRU 90 basis, and why it is not used for scoring.

Smaller items

Photon categories were mislabelled at importance-cell boundaries during development (fixed before production; zero untagged tracks since). The region-of-interest check lacked a buffer in front of a depth scan starting inside the phantom (no M1 run was affected; fixed for M2). Twice, process clean-up commands matched my own shell; one two-minute run was lost and archived with its reason.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths

  • Every layer has an independent check: analytic primary fluence with a measured tracking μ, thin slab, fluence-to-dose closure at 0.07%, analog against variance reduction, S1, S2, a seed-independent replicate, and a boundary-free dose.
  • Anomalies were run down, not explained away. Two were real Geant4 issues; one was statistics, settled by a pre-registered replicate.
  • Conservative uncertainties. The larger of two error estimates everywhere; targets met on that basis.
  • The design separates recall from computation (H3), and the layered grading shows where an answer fails, not only whether.
  • Sampled-event validity rules are physical and calibrated on the reference physics, so invalid AI events can be counted separately from distributional distance.
  • Full provenance. Any number on this page traces to a run manifest, a commit and a decision entry.

Weaknesses and open risks

  • One code so far. Until EGSnrc and the Livermore and Penelope lists run (M2), the reference is one code's physics and the resolution floor is unknown.
  • sw,air has no unique truth. The 1% basis spread is set by convention (D024); reviewers will ask, and the TRS-398 revision's basis still needs checking.
  • Source model A is idealized: no head scatter, no source size, no contamination electrons. PDD(5) and the scatter fraction will differ from a real unit, and models may answer for a real unit unless the prompt is explicit.
  • Open systematics: the scoring-boundary effect on D (±0.17%, tightening) and the Fano deviation (+0.16%, 2.7σ, step-size diagnostics pending).
  • External values unverified. NIST μ/ρ and μen/ρ could not be fetched from the container, so the plan's kerma-from-tables cross-check is not done yet.
  • The Doppler tolerance comes from the reference itself (99.9th percentile). It is physically motivated, but a definition from Compton-profile data would be harder to question.
  • One point, one depth. Generality waits for the V1 variations.
  • The AI side does not exist yet. The main risks ahead are prompt ambiguity, model-version drift and cost.

Where to publish

The publishable result is the AI comparison (M4–M5); M1 is its foundation. My recommendation: write the main paper for Medical Physics, submit an AAPM annual meeting abstract once the first AI results exist (or ESTRO, depending on timing), and decide on a benchmark-track version once the V1 variations are scoped. The plan lists the venue as an open decision because it changes the reporting format; the table says how.
