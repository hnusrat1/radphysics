Co-60 reference point · M4 AI runs · scored against Monte Carlo

Five fast models at the Co-60 reference point

R0: central axis, 5 cm depth, water, 10 × 10 cm², SSD 100 cm 125 conversations5 models × 3 conditionsrun 2026-10-12 re-scored 2026-10-29

Each model was asked for 33 quantities that follow the energy from the incident photon to the absorbed dose, five times per condition, and for 1,000 sampled photon interactions. Answers are scored against the study's Monte Carlo reference (Geant4 option4, boundary-free dose; D/Φ₀ = 5.3672e-12 Gy cm²).

What the runs show

  • The method decides the answer. The 17 tool answers built on a photon-transport simulation the model wrote have a median error of 0.8% in the scattered photon fluence and 0.5% in D/Φ₀. The 20 whose scatter was typed or assumed are off by 57% and 16%.
  • Which models simulated. Haiku in 10 of 10 tool runs, DeepSeek in 8 (two with major bugs), GPT-6 Luna in 2 (one compiled C++ run as accurate as Haiku, one with a major bug), Qwen in none. Gemini wrote no code.
  • Without tools, every model misses the dose. The closest A0 answer is Claude Haiku 5.5 at -2.6%. The common error is the same one the analytic tool answers make: too few multiply scattered, low-energy photons at the point.
  • What even the simulations get wrong. 19 of the 20 simulations never transport electrons, so electron directions fail everywhere. None models electron binding, and the photoelectric share comes out at about a third of the Monte Carlo value. 10 of 20 typed PDD(5) from memory instead of computing it.
  • Answers are not always what the code computed. Only 11 of 40 tool answers come entirely from the code's output, and 11 stated methods describe calculations the code did not do.
  • Sampled events. Under the bound-electron rule, Haiku's events (A0 and A1) and DeepSeek's A0 events are almost all physically valid; DeepSeek's A1 events are 13% invalid, Qwen's 51% and Gemini's 28–66%. Two samples match the Monte Carlo distribution: Haiku's A1 events and the one A1 call GPT-6 Luna answered. It declined the other 9 event calls, saying it cannot sample without a transport code.
  • Every model breaks at Layer 1 under the pre-set criterion. Shapes must match within three times the Monte Carlo's own half-sample distance (0.7 keV in W1 for the scattered spectrum). A simulated answer misses by about 5 keV and an assumed one by about 218 keV, and both count as a failure.
  • With physical shape tolerances, the tool answers separate. Under a revised criterion chosen after these runs (D038, exploratory here): W1 within 5% of the Monte Carlo mean energy (24 keV for the scattered spectrum), or 0.1 in cos θ. Claude Haiku 5.5 with tools (A1, A2) then breaks at Layer 2, on the photoelectric share of interactions, with every Layer 1 and Layer 3 shape passing and D/Φ₀ within 2%. Every other model and condition still breaks at Layer 1, mostly on the scattered-photon spectrum.

What the simulations included

  • All 20 set up the finite water cube, the diverging 10 × 10 cm² field and both Co-60 lines, and kept the Φ₀ normalization consistent.
  • Klein–Nishina Compton sampling in 18; Rayleigh with an angular distribution in 15; annihilation photons after pair production in 14; electron binding or Doppler broadening in 0.
  • Photon cutoff: none in 9, between 1 and 20 keV in the rest. Cross sections from the XCOM file in 11, from formulas or typed tables otherwise.
  • Electrons: a continuous-slowing-down spectrum built from stopping powers in 17, transported in 1. Dose as Kcol × β under charged-particle equilibrium in 16.

What the audit found

  • Three simulations had major bugs that the models did not notice: GPT-6 Luna processed interactions only inside the scoring cylinder (A2 r5); DeepSeek sampled Compton angles from a wrong rejection envelope, giving isotropic scatter (A2 r4), and built one answer on a 10,000-history run with a broken cylinder intersection (A1 r2).
  • GPT-6 Luna's and Qwen's analytic answers do not compute a buildup factor or a single-scatter integral: the scattered fluence is a constant the model chose (0.075 to 0.30 per Φ₀ for GPT-6 Luna; buildup factors from 1.04 to 1.45 for Qwen) and the spectrum is a typed shape.
  • Two answers claim a Monte Carlo that did not run (Qwen A1 r4; DeepSeek A1 r5, whose reply says it was "implemented and validated").
  • Unit and normalization errors explain the largest misses: a 1.602e-13 for 1.602e-10 in Qwen's electron fluence (×1,000), MeV/g to Gy conversions off by 10³ in its dose.
  • GPT-6 Luna compiled C++ with the system compiler in three R0 conversations. The sandbox was meant to be Python only; isolation held (D036). The compilers are now removed for later runs (D037), and the results below are shown with and without these conversations.

Read these results with

  • The models are each provider's fast, low-cost tier, not the most capable model (D030). Qwen3.7 Flash fills an open-weight slot but has no published weights.
  • Haiku runs with prompt caching to stay inside the $10 budget; the model sees the same prompts (D032).
  • Event answers count with 150 to 250 events; the prompt asks for 200 (D031).
  • Gemini was rate-limited by its upstream throughout; its four API-error conversations were rerun (D033, D035).
  • A malformed fenced JSON block gets a misleading retry message in this harness version (D034). It affects which answers are readable, not how readable answers score.
  • The Monte Carlo reference is final (M2, not provisional). Re-scoring against it after the merge with the M2 branch gave identical verdicts. Raw conversations are in ai/raw/M4-R0/; scores in analysis/results/M5_M4-R0.json.
