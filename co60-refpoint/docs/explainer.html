A short film about one photon

The life of a cobalt-60 photon

and whether an AI can follow it from the source to the dose it leaves in water.

Scene 1 · the source

Gamma is born when a cobalt-60 nucleus decays

Each decay sends out two photons: one at 1.1732 MeV and one at 1.3325 MeV. In our model the source is a point, and Gamma is equally likely to be either.

Scene 2 · the destination

Its target: 5 cm deep in a tank of water

Source 100 cm from the surface, a 10 × 10 cm² field, a point on the axis at 5 cm depth. Standards labs calibrate ion chambers at exactly this point, so it is one of the best-known spots in radiation dosimetry.

Scene 5 · how

A referee, and three ways to ask the AI

Monte Carlo referee

  • Geant4 11.4.3: option4, Livermore and Penelope physics
  • EGSnrc as an independent second code
  • Every photon and electron tracked, every number scored

AI models

  • Recall: the question only, no tools
  • Compute: plus a Python sandbox
  • Compute with data: plus NIST tables

If the referees disagree with each other by 0.3%, an AI that is 0.2% off counts as a tie, not a miss.

Scene 6 · building the referee

Fast, and honest about it

Photon splitting near the pointA photon heading for the scoring volume becomes 32 lighter copies, so rare interactions there get sampled 32 times.
Russian roulette far awayAn electron born too far away to reach the point survives 1 time in 20, and then counts 20 times.
25× fasterBoth tricks are unbiased by design; an unbiased brute-force run is queued to prove it.
Scene 7 · early results, first of ten batches (12 million photons)

What the referee sees at 5 cm

37%of the photons arriving have already scattered at least once (18% of the energy they carry)
99.7%of the energy handed to electrons comes from Compton scattering
346 keVmean starting energy of an electron: 587 keV from unscattered photons, 125 keV from scattered ones
5.35 × 10⁻¹² Gy cm²dose per unit photon fluence, D/Φ₀; 19% of it arrives through scattered photons

Spencer–Attix water-to-air ratio with ICRU 37 stopping powers: 1.1331. The value in the IAEA TRS-398 Code of Practice is 1.133.

Scene 8 · checking the referee

We caught the referee making mistakes too

Missing physicsA Geant4 speed-up skips Rayleigh scattering above 1.022 MeV, so Gamma could never scatter coherently. Switched off; Rayleigh is back.
Electron bookkeepingThe plan's linear-loss rule put dose 6.4% too high when rebuilt from the electron spectrum. Weighting by stopping power: 0.07%.
Bound electronsReal Compton electrons are not free: Doppler broadening moves the scattered energy by up to 134 keV from the textbook formula.
Water in Geant4Its density-effect correction runs about 0.5% low on stopping power near 1 MeV. Reported, and matched in the EGSnrc benchmark.
